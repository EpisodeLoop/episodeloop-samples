01d · Ranking blog article

LinkedIn Ads Cost 40x More But Deliver 10x Better Leads. Here's Why That Math Works.

Word count: ~1,150 Target keyword: intent-based LinkedIn ads funnel Slug: /linkedin-ads-intent-based-full-funnel-nico-kusterer

I've overseen more than 150 LinkedIn ad campaigns at Impactable, and I still hear the same objection almost every week: "LinkedIn is too expensive." I get why people say it. If you look at cost-per-click or CPM in isolation, LinkedIn can cost 40 times more than the other channels you're used to. But that's the wrong number to look at, and my conversation with Nico Kusterer on LinkedIn AdWise is one of the cleanest breakdowns I've done of why.

Nico is the founder of The One Media and a former LinkedIn Certified Marketing Expert. He came to LinkedIn from the Meta side, which is exactly why his perspective is useful — he's seen both worlds, and he built his expertise in what's still, frankly, an underserved market. Very few people run LinkedIn ads well. Most run them the way they'd run Meta, and that's where the money leaks out.

The most expensive mistake on LinkedIn

Here's the mistake Nico and I keep seeing, and it's a costly one: cold targeting paired with sales messaging. Marketers switch LinkedIn on, point it at a cold audience they've never warmed up, and hit them with "book a demo." On a platform where a click can cost 40x what you're used to, that combination burns budget faster than anything else you can do.

The problem isn't LinkedIn. The problem is treating the most expensive click in your stack like a bottom-of-funnel coupon. If you're going to pay a premium for the click, you'd better have a reason for that person to care before you ask them to buy.

The 16-week pipeline framework: start mid-funnel, prove conversion first

One of the most useful things Nico shared is a 16-week pipeline framework — and the counterintuitive part is where you start. Most people want to start at the top, run big awareness campaigns, and fill the funnel. Nico does the opposite. He starts mid-funnel, with a high-intent lead magnet, and proves conversion before scaling anything.

Why? Because if you can't convert the people already showing intent, spending more to pour cold traffic in at the top just makes the leak bigger. Prove the mid-funnel converts. Build your first retargeting audiences off that activity — company-tab, high-intent ABM audiences, and person-based audiences. Then, once you can see it working, you layer awareness campaigns on top to feed more of the right people into the machine.

I want to be honest the way Nico was: this framework works for specific clients, not everyone. If you don't have enough traffic or a real offer that converts, no framework saves you. But for the right B2B company, starting mid-funnel and earning the right to scale is exactly how you make expensive clicks pay for themselves.

How to build an intent-based full funnel

This is the core idea, and it's why LinkedIn's premium price can still deliver 10x better leads. The goal isn't reach. The goal is to identify intent at every single stage of the funnel.

Start at the account level. LinkedIn's company-engagement data — the company tab that's been around a year and a half or two now — lets you see which accounts are actually engaging. That's your first intent signal, and it's the one most advertisers ignore. From there you move to the person level: single-image ads to individuals inside those engaged accounts.

Each stage has its own intent signal. In the awareness stage, intent looks like video views or ungated document interactions — someone consuming your content is telling you something. In the next stage, it's engagement with person-level ads. You're not spraying and hoping; you're reading signals and moving people forward based on what they actually do. That's the difference between "expensive" and "efficient," even at the same CPC.

Thought Leader Ads deserve ~80% of your budget

If there's one tactical takeaway I'd put in bold, it's this: Thought Leader Ads outperform company-page posts, and Nico allocates something like 80% of budget to them for a reason. People trust people, not logos. A promoted post from a real person in your company — sharing a genuine point of view — earns attention that a company-page ad simply can't buy on LinkedIn anymore.

Pair that with the right format mix — text ads, spotlight ads, customer testimonials — and Lead Gen Forms to capture quality without waiting on your dev team to build landing pages. Lead Gen Forms remove friction on both sides: the prospect converts in-platform, and you don't lose weeks to development delays.

So, are LinkedIn Ads too expensive?

Back to the myth. LinkedIn ads look expensive on a cost-per-click basis and cheap on a cost-per-quality-lead basis. That's the whole reframe. If you measure the surface metric everyone obsesses over — CPC, CPM — you'll always conclude LinkedIn is overpriced. If you measure the thing that actually matters to your business — the cost of a lead that turns into pipeline and revenue — LinkedIn frequently wins, precisely because the targeting and the trust are better.

The 40x/10x line isn't a gimmick. It's a summary of a real trade-off: you pay more per touch, but with an intent-based full funnel and Thought Leader Ads doing the heavy lifting, the leads you get are dramatically better qualified. Expensive clicks, cheaper customers.

If your LinkedIn ads aren't producing pipeline right now, don't conclude the channel is broken. Look at your funnel. Are you starting mid-funnel and proving conversion, or dumping cold traffic on sales messaging? Are you reading intent at the account and person level, or buying reach? Are you leaning on Thought Leader Ads, or boosting company-page posts and wondering why nobody cares?
