02 · SEO Blog Article

How to Scale an Outbound SDR Team in 2026 (Without Over-Hiring)

Word count: ~1,300 Primary keyword: scale outbound SDR team 2026 Meta: ZoomInfo's Florin Tatulea on the SAM math that stops over-hiring, when to hire your first SDR, and what AI really changes about outbound.

I've watched a lot of teams scale outbound, and most of them make the same mistake before they ever place a single job ad. They size the team backwards. Somebody picks a revenue target, works out how many meetings that implies, divides by a per-rep number, and out pops a headcount. Then they go hire that many SDRs against a market nobody actually measured. That's how you end up with a full bench and an empty pipeline.

So on episode #33 of Outbound Kitchen I brought on Florin Tatulea — GTM Engineer in Residence at ZoomInfo, Loopio's first sales hire on a team that grew revenue 65x in six years, and the guy who built the outbound engine at Common Room. Florin has done this from zero more than once, and he doesn't hire on vibes. He hires on math.

The one number that reframes the whole thing

Here's the moment from our conversation I keep coming back to. On one motion, roughly 30,000 accounts looked addressable. It's the number that goes on the board slide. It's the number that justifies the headcount plan. And when Florin actually did the work of sizing the serviceable market — the accounts that fit the ICP, could buy, and were reachable — the real number was about 6,000.

Six thousand, not thirty thousand. That's an 80% haircut on the market you thought you had.

If you staff outbound against the 30,000 and the truth is 6,000, everything downstream breaks. Reps run out of good accounts and start working bad ones to hit activity. Your data on "what's working" gets polluted because half the pipeline was never a real fit. And you conclude your playbook is broken when actually your market map was fiction. The fix isn't a better cadence. It's doing the SAM math before you hire.

Don't hire an SDR too early

The second thing Florin is firm on: you don't need an SDR before roughly $1M ARR or before you have product-market fit. I know that's not what an ambitious founder wants to hear, because hiring a rep feels like progress. But an SDR is a scaling function, not a discovery function. If you don't yet know who buys, why they buy, and what makes them say yes, an SDR just burns your list faster while you're still figuring that out.

Founders should be doing outbound themselves in that phase — not because it's glamorous, but because the messaging, the ICP, and the objections you learn firsthand are the playbook you'll eventually hand a rep. Skip that and you're not delegating a system, you're delegating a guess.

Why he hires two SDRs at once

This one is counterintuitive and I love it. When it's time to hire, Florin hires two SDRs at once, not one. The reason is diagnostic: with a single rep, you can't tell whether a bad result is the person or the process. One rep struggles and you're stuck guessing. Two reps working the same motion give you a comparison. If both struggle, it's the process — the ICP, the list, the messaging, the offer. If one flies and one doesn't, it's the person. You've separated the two variables that every early outbound team confuses.

That's not a hiring tip. That's an experimental design. It's exactly the kind of thinking that separates teams who scale outbound from teams who just add to it.

What he looks for in a first SDR

We went deep on hiring, and Florin has four things he screens for in a first rep. The one worth calling out here is curiosity, because everyone claims to hire for it and almost nobody actually tests it. He doesn't take "I'm a curious person" as an answer. He looks at whether a candidate did real homework on the company and the market before the call, whether they ask questions that go a layer deeper than the job description, whether they poke at why something works. Curiosity you can observe beats curiosity someone asserts.

And then a framing I want every sales leader to steal: build the team like a sports team. Not five clones of your top performer. Complementary skill sets. The person who's relentless on the phone, the person who writes a surgical email, the person who's a researcher, the person who's a connector. A team of identical reps has identical blind spots. A team with complementary strengths covers for each other's gaps — and it's a lot more fun to manage.

What AI actually changes (and what it doesn't)

Because it's 2026, we had to talk about AI and the SDR. Here's the honest version. AI and GTM engineering genuinely change the shape of the team you build — more leverage per rep, more of the grunt work (research, list-building, prioritization, first-draft messaging) handled by agents and workflows, so a rep's time goes toward the things only a human can do.

But the "AI kills the SDR" narrative is overstated, and there's a simple tell: Anthropic, OpenAI, and Clay — the companies closest to the frontier of this technology — are still hiring SDRs. The people who best understand what AI can do are still betting on humans in the outbound seat. What changes is the job, not the existence of the job. The SDR of 2026 is more of a GTM engineer than a dialer.

The recipe, if you're scaling outbound this year

Pulling it together, here's what I took from Florin: do the SAM math before you hire — find your 6,000 before you staff for 30,000. Don't hire an SDR before ~$1M ARR or PMF; founders do outbound first to build the playbook they'll hand over. Hire two at once so you can separate the person from the process. Vet curiosity by observing it, not asking about it, and build a team like a sports team — complementary, not cloned. And let AI change the job, not scare you off the role; the frontier labs are still hiring SDRs.

Outbound at scale isn't about more reps and more activity. It's about measuring the real market, hiring against it deliberately, and building a team that covers its own blind spots. That's the difference between a full bench and a full pipeline.
