How to Scale an Outbound SDR Team in 2026 (Without Over-Hiring)

I've watched a lot of teams scale outbound, and most make the same mistake before they ever place a single job ad. They size the team backwards. Somebody picks a revenue target, works out how many meetings that implies, divides by a per-rep number, and out pops a headcount. Then they go hire that many SDRs against a market nobody actually measured. That's how you end up with a full bench and an empty pipeline.

So on episode #33 of Outbound Kitchen I brought on Florin Tatulea — GTM Engineer in Residence at ZoomInfo, Loopio's first sales hire on a team that grew revenue 65x in six years, and the guy who built the outbound engine at Common Room. He doesn't hire on vibes. He hires on math. If you're figuring out how to scale an outbound SDR team this year, this is the recipe.

The one number that reframes the whole thing

On one motion, roughly 30,000 accounts looked addressable — the number that goes on the board slide and justifies the headcount plan. When Florin actually sized the serviceable market — accounts that fit the ICP, could buy, and were reachable — the real number was about 6,000. That's an 80% haircut. Staff against 30,000 when the truth is 6,000 and everything downstream breaks: reps run out of good accounts and start working bad ones, your "what's working" data gets polluted, and you conclude your playbook is broken when your market map was fiction. The fix isn't a better cadence. It's doing the SAM math before you hire.

When to hire your first SDR (and when not to)

Florin is firm: you don't need an SDR before roughly $1M ARR or product-market fit. Hiring a rep feels like progress, but an SDR is a scaling function, not a discovery function. If you don't yet know who buys, why, and what makes them say yes, an SDR just burns your list faster. Founders should do outbound themselves in that phase — the messaging, ICP, and objections you learn firsthand are the playbook you'll hand a rep. Skip it and you're delegating a guess.

Why he hires two SDRs at once

When it's time to hire, Florin hires two SDRs at once. The reason is diagnostic: with one rep you can't tell whether a bad result is the person or the process. Two reps on the same motion give you a comparison. Both struggle → it's the process (ICP, list, messaging, offer). One flies, one doesn't → it's the person. That's not a hiring tip, it's experimental design.

What he looks for in a first SDR

Florin screens for four things; the one worth calling out is curiosity, because everyone claims to hire for it and almost nobody tests it. He doesn't take "I'm a curious person" as an answer — he looks at whether a candidate did real homework, asks a layer deeper, pokes at why something works. And a framing to steal: build the team like a sports team — complementary skill sets, not five clones. Identical reps have identical blind spots.

What AI actually changes (and what it doesn't)

AI and GTM engineering genuinely change the shape of the team — more leverage per rep, more grunt work (research, list-building, prioritization, first-draft messaging) handled by agents and workflows. But the "AI kills the SDR" narrative is overstated, and there's a tell: Anthropic, OpenAI, and Clay — the companies closest to the frontier — are still hiring SDRs. What changes is the job, not the existence of the job. The SDR of 2026 is more of a GTM engineer than a dialer.

The recipe, if you're scaling outbound this year

Do the SAM math before you hire. Don't hire an SDR before ~$1M ARR or PMF. Hire two at once to separate the person from the process. Vet curiosity by observing it, and build a team like a sports team. And let AI change the job, not scare you off the role. Outbound at scale isn't more reps and more activity — it's measuring the real market, hiring against it deliberately, and building a team that covers its own blind spots. That's the difference between a full bench and a full pipeline.
