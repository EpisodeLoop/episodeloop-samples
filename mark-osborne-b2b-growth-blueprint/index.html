When to Hire a CFO: The Revenue Milestones That Signal It's Time

I started The B2B Growth Blueprint to dig into the systems and processes behind durable growth — the unglamorous machinery that separates companies that scale from companies that just stay busy. One of the most common questions I hear from founders is deceptively simple: when do I actually need to hire a CFO? My conversation with Brit Summerill, a Partner at NOW CFO who's spent 14 years rebuilding broken financial systems for companies from startup through $60M and beyond, is the clearest answer I've put on the show.

Here's the line from Brit that reframed the whole thing for me: "Nobody comes to me for accounting, they're coming to me for visibility." Knowing when to hire a CFO isn't really about bookkeeping. It's about whether you can see your business clearly enough to make good decisions — and whether the story your numbers tell will hold up the day someone with a checkbook comes to look.

Most founders run the whole company on one number

In the early days, you manage by bank balance. It works, and honestly it's the right instinct when you're small. But as revenue climbs, that cash-basis, gut-driven approach quietly stacks up risk. You don't know your true margins. You have no internal controls. And your books won't survive diligence. Brit's description stuck with me: "They're really just bootstrapping and flying by the seat of their pants, and there's duct tape on the wheels." Nothing forces the issue until it's expensive — a funding round, an M&A conversation, a private-equity call — and then the cleanup can derail the whole deal.

The revenue milestones where you outgrow bank-balance thinking

The most useful part of this conversation is that Brit put actual numbers on when to change your system. Around $5M in revenue, cash-basis bookkeeping stops working — move to accrual accounting, add revenue recognition, and track basic KPIs. For a lot of founders this is the moment a fractional CFO earns its keep: visibility without a full-time hire. Around $10M, you need controllers and real internal controls, because the surface area for error has grown past what one person watching the bank account can manage. And here's the trap: founders who wait until $20M create expensive cleanup and avoidable risk. They don't save money by delaying — they defer a smaller bill into a much bigger one.

The hidden costs of flying blind

The cost of poor visibility isn't just stress. Without visibility into true margins, companies waste resources building against their weakest products. They get denied credit lines and resort to expensive hard-money loans. They overpay taxes and penalties on multi-state activity. And when controls are missing, they expose themselves to internal theft. Brit had a phrase for the warning sign: "growing broke." Busier than ever, revenue climbing, and yet less and less cash. That's not a cash-flow hiccup — it's a visibility problem wearing a cash-flow costume.

Why accounting kills deals "every time"

Every founder thinking about an eventual exit needs to hear this. Brit was blunt: "There's a few things that'll kill a deal. One of them's accounting. Every time." Roughly 50% of owners are forced to sell when unprepared, and around 70% of small-business M&A deals fall through. The two biggest deal-killers: messy books that don't tell a clean story, and founder dependency with no succession plan. His line I'll be repeating: "There's no bigger way to lose a deal than to walk in the room not knowing what your company's really worth."

What to actually do about it

The fixes are the same whether or not you ever plan to sell. Treat your books as if you could be audited tomorrow. Automate manual processes. Benchmark your margins against your industry. And build a team that can run the business without you. The most valuable businesses aren't the biggest — they're the ones with clean books, strong margins, and systems that don't depend on the founder grinding it out. So the honest answer to "when should I hire a CFO?" is: invest in visibility before you need it, not after a deal is already on the table.

If your business is growing but you can't clearly see your margins, your risk, or what you'd be worth to a buyer, that's exactly the foundation we help build at Modern Revenue Strategies — systems, not hustle.
