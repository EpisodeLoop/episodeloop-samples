02 · SEO Blog Article

How to Create Awareness Among Strategic Accounts (Before the Buying Group Even Starts Searching)

Word count: ~900 Primary keyword: strategic account awareness Meta: How to create awareness among strategic accounts and measure it — Kerry Cunningham (6sense) & Matt Heinz on the buyer's journey, from the Full-Funnel B2B Marketing Show.

Every B2B marketer will tell you awareness matters. Almost nobody can tell you what they'd measure to prove it. That gap — between "awareness is important" and "awareness is measured" — was the whole conversation in episode 184 of the Full-Funnel B2B Marketing Show, with Kerry Cunningham of 6sense and Matt Heinz of Heinz Marketing.

Here's what we landed on, and what it means for your strategic accounts.

The decision happens before the call

The research Kerry has published keeps pointing the same direction: buyers complete most of their journey before they engage with sellers. The buyer's experience with your sales reps influences the purchase decision — and that experience starts long before the first call. Every interaction is input: the content they read, the ads they saw, the reviews they checked, the rep they met at the event.

The uncomfortable implication is that by the time your pipeline reports a new opportunity, the position was already won or lost. Awareness work isn't the warm-up act. It's the reason the funnel exists.

What works today

So what actually creates awareness among strategic accounts in 2026? Three things came out of the conversation:

1. Show up before the search starts. Awareness is being the vendor the buying group already knows when they begin evaluating. That means your content, your channels, and your community presence need to reach the account before they're mid-evaluation — in their channels, on their terms.

2. Use signals to aim the effort. You can't blanket the market anymore. Intent data and account signals tell you which strategic accounts are in motion, so awareness effort goes where it compounds.

3. Define awareness operationally. Not "brand lift" as an abstraction — but the concrete things that precede pipeline: visits from target accounts, share of first engagement, and what your reps hear in discovery conversations.

How to measure it

This is where most programs fall apart. If you can't measure awareness, you can't improve it — and in B2B, awareness is improvable. It just has to be designed.

The practical measurement stack: track intent-signal coverage on your target account list, measure share of first engagement (are you the first vendor they engaged with?), and build a lightweight rep-reporting loop so discovery calls feed back what the market actually thinks of you. None of it is perfect. All of it beats "we feel like our brand is getting stronger."

The full-funnel trap

The easiest way to sabotage this work is the classic funnel fiction: awareness at the top, demand at the bottom, sequential. Full-funnel marketing means awareness keeps working while demand converts. They're not phases — they're simultaneous layers. The teams that win understand that the top of the funnel isn't a stage; it's a constant.

That's the practical throughline of episode 184, and it's why we brought in guests who think about this for a living. Kerry's research on the buyer journey and Matt's decades in B2B demand together give you the why and the how.

If you're trying to grow pipeline from strategic accounts — and who isn't — start with the measurement question. Answer it, and the awareness work stops being a vibe and starts being a system.
