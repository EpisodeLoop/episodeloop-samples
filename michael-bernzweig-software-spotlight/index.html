02 · SEO Blog Article

What a 20-Year Telecom Pivot Teaches Every B2B SaaS About Reinvention

Word count: ~1,100 Primary keyword: B2B SaaS reinvention / industry restructuring Meta: LoopUp's Steve Flavell on a 20-year reinvention from conference calls to global telecom — and what it teaches B2B SaaS leaders navigating structural industry change.

There's a version of the pivot story that founders love: the overnight reinvention, the single decisive moment where a company sees the future and changes everything. On this week's Software Spotlight, Steve Flavell, co-founder and co-CEO of LoopUp, told a different version — the one that actually happens. A 20-year journey from conference call software to global telecommunications provider, through a pandemic that should have killed the category, a structural industry restructuring, and the slow, unglamorous work of building the future one customer at a time.

It's the most instructive conversation I've had in a while, and the lessons apply far beyond telecom.

The category can collapse while the company survives

LoopUp started in conference calling — a category that looked bulletproof until it wasn't. The pandemic didn't just reduce demand for conference calls; it rewired how businesses communicate entirely. For most companies in that position, the story ends there. LoopUp's story didn't, and Steve was clear about why: they treated the collapse as a signal to reinvent around the customer's new reality, not as a reason to defend the old product.

The lesson for B2B SaaS is uncomfortable but direct. If your category is being restructured, the companies that move early define the new market. The ones that wait for certainty are the ones that disappear.

The PSTN switch-off is a strategy moment, not a technical one

One of the most fascinating threads in the conversation was the PSTN switch-off — the end of the traditional public switched telephone network. On paper it's a technical infrastructure change. In practice it's a strategic moment. When the old infrastructure disappears, the plumbing of an entire industry is up for grabs. LoopUp positioned itself as a global telecom provider precisely because the old model was ending.

There's a version of this moment coming for every software category. When the underlying infrastructure changes — AI assistants, platform integrations, new distribution models — the incumbents who treat it as a technical footnote lose to the ones who treat it as a strategy question.

Meet customers where they already work

The other shift Steve walked through is the integration of telecom services with platforms like Teams. Customers aren't going to a phone company anymore; they're working inside platforms that should carry the telecom experience to them. The winners in this restructuring are the ones who meet customers where they work, not the ones who make customers come to them.

For B2B SaaS, this is the integration lesson that keeps compounding. Your product doesn't win by being the destination. It wins by being present in the tools your customers already use every day.

Governance is the quiet advantage

The panel Q&A that followed the interview surfaced something most founders never think about: governance checkpoints for restructuring, and strategic adaptation in highly regulated regions. When you're reshaping a business, the pace of decision-making matters as much as the direction. Regulated markets force discipline — and that discipline turns out to be an advantage when the market gets messy.

The patience that compounds

Twenty years is a long time to keep reinventing. What struck me most about Steve's story wasn't any single decision — it was the willingness to keep making them. Founders love the idea of the one bold move. The reality is a marathon of small strategic decisions, made consistently, through good years and bad.

If you're building a B2B SaaS company in a category that's being restructured — and most are — this episode is worth an hour of your time. The full conversation, including the panel Q&A on AI integration and telecom strategy, is live now.
