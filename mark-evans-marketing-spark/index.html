02 · SEO Blog Article

Agentic AI Strategy: Should You Bolt On AI or Reimagine the Business?

Word count: ~1,100 Primary keyword: agentic AI strategy for executives Meta: Blaine Mathieu on why agentic AI is a strategy challenge, not a tech project — and how B2B leaders should decide between retrofitting and reimagining.

Every company I talk to is adding AI to something. A chatbot here, a summarizer there, an efficiency gain buried in a spreadsheet. And most of it is working — marginally. Costs edge down, productivity edges up, and nobody has to make a hard decision.

Blaine Mathieu thinks that's the trap.

Blaine is a veteran of technology, strategy, product, and executive leadership, and the author of The River Doesn't Wait: A Senior Executive's Guide to Navigating the Surge in Agentic AI. On the latest episode of Marketing Spark, he pushed a question that most of us avoid: should AI improve how the company already operates, or should it become the catalyst for reimagining the business?

That's the question behind the phrase "agentic AI strategy" — and it's a strategy problem, not a technology one.

The bolt-on default

Most companies take the retrofit path. We keep the same business model, the same org chart, the same go-to-market motion, and we drop AI in on top. Faster emails. Faster code. Faster support tickets.

Bolt-on AI has real value. It's low-risk, measurable, and it pays for itself quickly. If all you want is a 10% efficiency gain, bolt-on AI delivers it.

The problem is what it doesn't do: it doesn't change how you compete. If your competitor reimagines their business around agentic AI while you merely speed up your old one, the efficiency gap closes fast — and then the structural gap is all that's left.

Reimagining is a strategy decision

Blaine's point is that agentic AI isn't another software implementation. It's a corporate strategy challenge, because agentic systems don't just execute tasks — they make decisions within a workflow. Once a system starts deciding, the question stops being "how do we automate this process?" and becomes "what should this business be?"

That's uncomfortable, because it puts the decision in the boardroom. You can delegate a technology implementation. You can't delegate reimagining what your company does and why it matters.

How to decide where to focus your AI investments

So where do you start? The episode breaks it down into a practical filter:

  • Where is the bottleneck? The most expensive constraint in your business is the first place AI should go — but only if the fix changes the constraint, not just the speed.
  • Where is the differentiation? Focus AI investment where it changes how you compete — not where it just trims cost on something nobody notices.
  • Where does the decision-making live? If the process you're automating contains judgment calls that define your brand, pricing, or service, that's a reimagine candidate, not a retrofit.

Redesigning work beats speeding it up

The sharpest line in the conversation: competitive advantage is more likely to come from redesigning work than from making existing processes faster.

Speed compresses. Everyone gets the same tools within a quarter or two. But a redesigned workflow — one where the process itself is different because the AI is in it — is much harder to copy, because it's tangled up with how you're organized, how you hire, and how you make decisions.

That's the real agentic AI strategy: not "how do we use AI," but "what would this company look like if it were built today with agentic AI available?" The companies that ask that question are the ones that don't get left behind by the surge.

What senior leaders should do this quarter

You don't need to reimagine everything at once. But you do need to start asking the question.

  • Pick one process where AI is already being discussed and ask: are we retrofitting, or are we reimagining?
  • Take one AI investment decision to the executive team and frame it as strategy, not tech.
  • Read the room on agentic AI — it's moving from hype to deployment, and the deployment decisions are being made now.

The river doesn't wait, as Blaine's title puts it. Neither does the competitive window.

This is exactly the conversation we go deep on in the latest episode of Marketing Spark. If you're a B2B leader deciding where AI fits in your company, this one is worth 40 minutes.
