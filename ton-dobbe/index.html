23 B2B SaaS CEOs, One Question, and the Advice That Actually Lasts

Three hundred episodes ago, I started The Remarkable SaaS Podcast with a simple belief. The founders quietly building genuinely valuable software companies have more to teach us than the loudest voices in the room. Three hundred episodes later, I'm more convinced than ever.

So for this milestone, I didn't want to hand the mic to a single guest. Instead, I went back through the last 100 episodes and pulled out the one thing I ask every single guest at the end of our conversation: what's a Do, or a Don't, you'd share with other SaaS entrepreneurs?

Here's what struck me when I lined up 23 of those answers side by side. Almost none of them are tactics. They're not about growth loops or pricing pages or the perfect onboarding flow. They're about how you think, how you treat people, and how you keep yourself honest over a very long journey.

The Don'ts that cut deepest

The very first one sets the tone. Neta Meidav, CEO of Vault Platform, says: "Don't take other entrepreneurs' advice on face value." There's a lovely irony in that being the opening line of an advice compilation, and it's exactly the point. Advice is context-dependent. What worked for someone else, in another market, at another stage, might quietly steer you wrong.

Melissa Kwan, CEO of eWebinar, offers one that too few founders are willing to say out loud: "Don't start a business that you hate, even if it has success." Success in the wrong thing doesn't set you free. It chains you to something you resent, with more resources and more people depending on it.

And then there's Sunny Han of Fulcrum: "Don't allow comfort to take over." This is the one I see catch experienced founders most often. Comfort doesn't announce itself as a mistake. It feels like you've finally made it, right up until a hungrier competitor proves you haven't.

Christian Owens of Paddle adds a subtler operational one: don't carry two different interpretations of what a good outcome looks like. Misalignment on the definition of "good" is how teams row hard in opposite directions and call it effort.

The Do's that stick

On the other side of the ledger, the through-line is clarity and ownership.

Patrick Woods, CEO of Orbit, says to aggressively communicate and keep everyone on the same page. In a distributed, fast-moving company, over-communication isn't noise. It's the thing that prevents a hundred small, expensive misunderstandings.

Guillaume Moubeche of Lempire offers the most practical advice in the whole set: "Do document as early as possible." It's not glamorous. It never makes the highlight reel. But every founder who skipped it ended up paying for it later, usually at the worst possible moment.

Ryan Falkenberg, CEO of CLEVVA, keeps it to three words: own the outcome. Not the task, not the effort, the outcome. It's a small shift in language that changes how an entire team shows up.

The one that outlasts the rest

If I had to pick the single answer that captures 300 episodes of conversations, it's Neta Meidav's second contribution: "Be human. Be a mensch."

Here's why it lands. You can out-execute a competitor on features. You can out-raise them, out-market them, out-ship them. What you cannot do is out-execute being someone people don't want to work with, buy from, or build alongside. Over a long enough timeline, being decent compounds like nothing else on the cap table.

Why I keep asking the question

Three hundred episodes in, I keep ending every conversation with that same Do-or-Don't question for one reason. The answers strip away the performance. When you ask a founder for their single most valuable lesson, they don't reach for the growth hack. They reach for the thing that cost them the most to learn.

That's what this episode is. 23 lessons that cost real founders real time, distilled into something you can absorb in one sitting.

If you want to go deeper on what separates the remarkable SaaS companies from the rest, that's exactly what I wrote The Remarkable Effect about. But if you just want the field notes from 23 people who've been where you are, episode #300 is your cheat sheet.
