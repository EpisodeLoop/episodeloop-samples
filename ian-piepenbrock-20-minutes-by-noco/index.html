02 · SEO Blog Article

The Website Is the New Marketing Team: What Ploy's Bryant Chou Means for B2B SaaS

Word count: ~900 Primary keyword: agent-first website platform B2B SaaS Meta: Webflow co-founder Bryant Chou's Ploy is a bet that the website itself should run your marketing. We tested the premise for 60 minutes — here's what holds up.

Twelve years ago, Bryant Chou helped build Webflow on a simple thesis: making websites easy to build would unlock a generation of companies that could design, launch, and iterate without waiting on anyone. Twelve years later, he's betting again — this time that the website itself should run your marketing.

I invited him on the show for a full 60 minutes to put that premise under pressure. Not because I was sold, but because I wasn't. An all-in-one, agent-first platform sounds either inevitable or like yet another tool that claims it just killed an industry. The conversation was more interesting than both options.

Agent-first, without spreading thin

The first thing Bryant pushed back on was the caricature of "AI does everything." Ploy's bet is narrower and smarter: the AI should orchestrate — pull in your content, your brand, your design system — while the humans stay in charge of what the output means. Agent-first doesn't mean agent-everything. It means the machine does the assembling; you do the directing.

That distinction matters for every B2B SaaS team looking at these tools. The teams that win won't be the ones with the most AI. They'll be the ones who keep a tight design system and let the agent execute against it. Trust, product, and outcomes still win in the age of AI slop — the tools just changed.

The live demo changed my mind

The part I was most skeptical about was the demo. Ploy's "Website Slurper" pulled in an existing site and rebuilt it live: clean code, real design system, on-brand output. It wasn't magic. It was a head start — and that's the honest framing.

The realistic use case for most B2B SaaS companies isn't replacing your site. It's the reverse proxy: keep Webflow, add AEO pages on top. Same CMS, same stack, plus pages built for the AI answer engines that are quietly becoming the new top of funnel. You don't need to rip anything out to start getting found where your buyers actually search now.

Your website as a marketing brain

The most interesting idea in the episode was the long-term one: the website as a marketing brain. Account-based marketing and visitor enrichment running on your own domain — which accounts visit, what they read, what they come back for — so the site becomes a tool that feeds your pipeline, not a destination that waits for traffic.

For agencies, that's an opportunity, not a threat. The tool makes the builds faster; the humans still win the accounts. "Why agencies win with Ploy" earned its own chapter for a reason.

What it means for designers and developers

Bryant's advice for the people currently worried about their tools: don't run from them. Learn what agentic tools actually change about your work — and then make them yours. The designers and developers who adapt stop fighting the machine and start directing it. The ones who don't are the ones who get left behind.

That's the optimistic throughline of the whole episode, and it's why we let this one run long. Building a startup in the AI era is strange — but the people doing it well are more optimistic than the timeline suggests.
